# Health and safety guidance

General information only. PawPal+ is not a substitute for a veterinarian and never chooses medication doses.

## Medication reminders

If a pet takes prescribed medication, set a reminder at the time your vet gave you. PawPal+ cannot know the dose or timing, so it adds one placeholder reminder: confirm the time and dose on the vet's label.

## Signs that need a vet right away
Contact a veterinarian or an emergency animal hospital immediately if a pet has difficulty breathing, collapses, has a seizure, is bleeding heavily, has a swollen hard belly, vomits repeatedly or vomits blood, cannot urinate, or has been hit by a car. Do not wait to see whether the problem passes.

## Foods and substances that are poisonous to pets
Chocolate, xylitol sweetener, grapes and raisins, onions and garlic are dangerous for dogs and cats, and lilies are extremely toxic to cats. Human medicines and antifreeze are also very dangerous. If a pet may have swallowed any of these, contact a vet or a poison control line straight away rather than waiting for symptoms.

## Poison emergencies
In the United States you can call ASPCA Animal Poison Control at (888) 426-4435 or the Pet Poison Helpline at (855) 764-7661, though fees may apply. Have the pet's weight, what was swallowed and how much ready. Phone numbers can change, so check them against your vet's advice.
